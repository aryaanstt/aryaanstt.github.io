About the data

  • Hotspots: NASA FIRMS, MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra + Aqua), yearly country archive for Indonesia. Kept: type 0 (presumed vegetation fire) and confidence ≥ 80. NASA promotes full and open use of its data (MODIS MCD14ML, doi:10.5067/FIRMS/MODIS/MCD14ML).
  • Rainfall: CHIRPS v2.0 monthly (Funk et al. 2015; public domain), via the IRI/LDEO Data Library, averaged over each province.
  • ENSO: NOAA Climate Prediction Center Oceanic Niño Index (public domain).
  • Provinces: geoBoundaries IDN ADM1 (34 provinces, 2017 boundaries; ODbL 1.0, © OpenStreetMap contributors).

Read with care

  • A hotspot is a 1 km pixel where the satellite saw a fire at the moment it passed over. Smoke, clouds and short fires between passes are missed, and one large fire can make many hotspots.
  • Correlation is not causation: dry seasons and El Niño go with more hotspots, but most fires in Indonesia are lit by people, so land use and policy matter too.
