How much does NO₂ over Java drop during Lebaran?

Lebaran (Eid al-Fitr) is Indonesia's largest annual movement of people. Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) comes mostly from burning fuel and lasts only hours in the air, so it reacts quickly when traffic and industry slow down. This dashboard shows how the Sentinel-5P tropospheric NO₂ column changed over Java's 117 regencies and cities during Lebaran, 2019–2025. Each year is compared with normal days in the same season of that year, because Eid moves about 11 days earlier every year.

How the numbers are made

  • Data: daily Sentinel-5P TROPOMI tropospheric NO₂ column (COPERNICUS/S5P/OFFL/L3_NO2), averaged per regency and city (FAO GAUL 2015 level 2) in Google Earth Engine. Negative retrievals are removed before averaging.
  • Periods: homecoming H−7 to H0, return H+1 to H+7, combined H−7 to H+7, where H0 is Eid day. Reference (normal days): H−60 to H−31 and H+30 to H+59 of the same year.
  • NO₂ column: the mean tropospheric column in the chosen window, in ×10⁻⁵ mol/m². Values range from about 0.4 in rural areas to 20 over Jakarta, so the map colours use a logarithmic scale. The 7-year view is the mean of the seven yearly values.
  • Anomaly: (Lebaran − reference) / reference × 100%, per region and year. The 7-year mean is the mean of the seven yearly anomalies. The Java value is the unweighted mean over the 117 regions.
  • Significance: Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the seven yearly values, exact distribution. DKI Jakarta in the key numbers is the whole province as one area; on the map it appears as its five cities.
  • Limits: the satellite sees the whole tropospheric column at about 13:30 local time, not ground-level air, and clouds leave gaps (grey regions have no valid data in that window). Polygons are simplified for the web.
