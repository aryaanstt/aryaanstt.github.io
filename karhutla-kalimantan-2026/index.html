Kalimantan's 2026 fire season: where it burned, where the smoke went, and what was in it

A very strong El Niño made 2026 one of the driest years on record in Kalimantan, and fires spread across its peatlands. The map below shows where satellites saw fires each month, and you can switch on layers to see the smoke: a gas (carbon monoxide), particles (Aerosol Index), the air-quality level people breathed (PM2.5), and the paths the wind carried the smoke along. Fires did not start in August. There were fires from January to July, but they were small; % of Kalimantan's 2026 fire detections came after 1 August, so slide the month to August-October to see the main season.

The two ocean "switches" behind dry years (°C)

El Niño index (Niño 3.4, Pacific) Indian Ocean Dipole (DMI)

Above the dotted line (+0.5 °C) the Pacific is in El Niño. A positive Indian Ocean Dipole cools the sea near Sumatra. Both pull rain away from western Indonesia; in 2026 both were on.

Data and limits

  • Fires: NASA FIRMS VIIRS S-NPP 375 m (near-real-time after June 2026). NASA open data; please credit LANCE/FIRMS.
  • CO and Aerosol Index: Copernicus Sentinel-5P; PM2.5: CAMS model analysis; both via Google Earth Engine (Copernicus licence).
  • Smoke paths: simple trajectories with NOAA GFS winds via Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0), a simplified version of what HYSPLIT does.
  • Rain: CHIRPS (public domain). Ocean indices: NOAA CPC and NOAA OISST (public domain).
  • City air quality: CAMS via Open-Meteo; airport reports via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet. Outlines: Natural Earth, geoBoundaries.
  • A fire detection is a hot pixel, not a burned area. Clouds and thick smoke hide some fires.
  • PM2.5 here is a model (~40 km), not a measurement, and includes city pollution.
  • Smoke paths use one height and coarse winds: they show the main direction, not smoke amounts. Winds were only downloaded for July-October.
  • Nothing here blames any company or person for fires.
